# Research Paper Report for 2026-02-01:2026-02-28

## Overall Summary

This collection of 18 research papers published during February 2026 reveals a landscape in which large language models (LLMs) and AI agent systems are rapidly maturing across healthcare, chemistry, materials science, environmental monitoring, and communications. Several dominant themes emerge: the deployment of LLM-powered agents for domain-specific decision support, the creation of rigorous benchmarks to evaluate these systems, critical reviews of adoption challenges, and the development of specialized foundation models.

**Agent-driven scientific discovery and clinical support** is a central theme. Papers [1] and [2] exemplify how LLM agents are being integrated into public health surveillance and drug repurposing pipelines, respectively. [1] demonstrates that a multi-model evaluation of four LLMs across avian influenza scenarios reveals significant variance in factual accuracy (ranging from 82.4% to 91.2%), with Gemini 1.5 Pro leading, and highlights the importance of role-aware benchmarking for equitable AI deployment in public health. [2] proposes an LLM-based agent that automates evidence synthesis from biomedical literature to prioritize Alzheimer's therapeutics, bridging the gap between computational predictions and in vivo validation. Meanwhile, [3] and [4] push agents into materials science and chemistry: [3] introduces a "Catalysis AI Agent" that couples large-scale data with LLMs to discover universal design principles for Cu-based single-atom alloy catalysts, while [4] demonstrates LLM-assisted synthesis of covalent organic frameworks, achieving a 350% increase in crystallinity through a structured two-cycle approach called LFAST. [5] describes a deployed multimodal sleep health agent ("Hang Hao Meng") that has triaged over four million individuals, showcasing real-world scalability.

**Benchmarking** emerges as a critical concern. [6] evaluates two agentic AI systems (OpenManus and Manus) across clinical decision benchmarks, finding only modest accuracy gains over baseline LLMs despite substantially increased computational costs (>10× token usage). [7] introduces CodeSafetyBench for evaluating ethical safety in code generation, demonstrating that educational feedback outperforms simple refusal in reducing harmful outputs. [8] examines LLM performance in soft tissue sarcoma tumor board decisions. [9] uncovers a Dunning-Kruger-like effect in multilingual fact-checking, where smaller models exhibit overconfidence despite lower accuracy, with particularly pronounced performance gaps for non-English languages and claims from the Global South. [10] benchmarks four reasoning-enhanced LLMs on the Japanese National Medical Examination, with all models exceeding 95% accuracy—Gemini 2.5 Pro achieving 97.2%—but struggling with image-based and complex clinical questions. [11] presents RareArena, the largest rare disease diagnostic benchmark, where GPT-4o achieves the best performance across 4597 rare diseases.

**Reviews** [12] and [13] offer critical perspectives: [12] surveys LLM applications in otolaryngology, noting that most research remains limited to feasibility studies and calling for open-source development and clinical trial-like validation. [13] examines the tension between engagement and therapeutic alliance in AI psychotherapy, raising bioethical concerns.

**Foundation model development** is represented by [14], which demonstrates that fine-tuning open-source LLMs on comprehensive domain data significantly improves specialization in anaerobic digestion; [15], which proposes a foundation-model-empowered framework achieving 95.6% compression ratio for real-time video conferencing; [16], which introduces DrugLLM with a novel Functional Group Tokenization strategy for molecular optimization, surpassing GPT-4; [17], which presents AquaticCLIP, a vision-language model for underwater scene understanding trained on 2 million image-text pairs; and [18], which proposes State-wise Linear Modulation (SLim) for steering LLM internal states to control emotional and stylistic attributes in outputs.

Collectively, these papers illustrate that while LLM capabilities are advancing rapidly, significant challenges remain in factual reliability, computational efficiency, equitable multilingual performance, and safe clinical deployment.

## AI Agents

This category encompasses five papers that deploy LLM-based agents across diverse domains—from public health surveillance and drug discovery to materials chemistry and sleep medicine. A unifying theme is the use of LLMs not merely as question-answering systems but as autonomous or semi-autonomous agents capable of multi-step reasoning, literature mining, evidence synthesis, and personalized decision support.

[1] evaluates four frontier LLMs (ChatGPT-4o, Grok, Gemini 1.5 Pro, DeepSeek R1) for avian influenza risk communication, finding Gemini 1.5 Pro achieves the highest factual accuracy at 91.2% while revealing model-specific communicative orientations—Gemini excels in empathetic pedagogical reasoning, Grok in implementation guidance, ChatGPT-4o in legal-normative clarity, and DeepSeek R1 in structural analysis. [2] takes a different approach by using an LLM-based agent to automate evidence synthesis from biomedical literature for Alzheimer's drug repurposing, integrating three computational methods (TxGNN, CompGCN, RLR) into a unified candidate list refined through transparent selection criteria. While [1] focuses on risk communication across stakeholders, [2] addresses the computational-experimental translation gap in drug discovery. [3] advances the agent paradigm into catalysis science, coupling a "Catalysis AI Agent" with a Digital Catalysis Platform to discover a universal structural descriptor (φ) for Cu-based single-atom alloy catalysts, demonstrating how domain-specific data and LLM reasoning can reveal fundamental design principles. [4] introduces the LLM For Accelerated Synthesis Technique (LFAST), employing a deep research agent within ChatGPT to accelerate covalent organic framework crystallization—reducing timelines from months to weeks and achieving 350% improvement in crystallinity. Unlike [3]'s focus on descriptor discovery, [4] emphasizes automated experimental execution coupled with high-throughput analysis. Finally, [5] presents "Hang Hao Meng," a deployed multimodal sleep health agent leveraging digital-human interfaces, which has served over four million individuals, representing the most operationally scaled agent system in this collection.

## Benchmarks

The six benchmark papers collectively address a critical gap in the field: the systematic, reproducible evaluation of LLM and AI agent capabilities across clinically and ethically relevant tasks. These papers range from broad multi-benchmark evaluations of agentic systems to highly specialized diagnostic assessments.

[6] provides perhaps the most sobering assessment in this collection, evaluating OpenManus (open-source, Llama-4-based) and Manus (proprietary, planner-executor-verifier architecture) across three benchmark families—AgentClinic, MedAgentsBench, and Humanity's Last Exam. Despite access to advanced tools including web browsing and code execution, agent systems achieved only modest accuracy gains (e.g., 60.3% on AgentClinic MedQA) while consuming >10× tokens and >2× latency compared to baseline LLMs. [7] shifts focus to AI safety in code generation, introducing CodeSafetyBench with 1,050 harmful request cases and demonstrating that a triadic response structure (harmful outputs, refusals, educational feedback) trained via SFT and DPO reduces harmful responses by up to 48%. While [6] emphasizes clinical reasoning accuracy, [7] addresses the ethical dimensions of LLM outputs. [8] evaluates LLMs in soft tissue sarcoma tumor board decisions, a specialized clinical scenario where multidisciplinary consensus is critical. [9] reveals systemic biases in multilingual fact-checking: smaller models show Dunning-Kruger-like overconfidence, and performance gaps are most pronounced for non-English languages and Global South claims, highlighting equity concerns that parallel findings in [1] about context-sensitive AI deployment. [10] benchmarks four reasoning-enhanced LLMs (GPT-5, Grok-4, Claude Opus 4.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro) on the Japanese National Medical Examination, all exceeding 95% accuracy, with Gemini 2.5 Pro leading at 97.2%—yet all models struggle with image-based items and complex clinical reasoning, suggesting fundamental limitations that [6] also identifies. [11] introduces RareArena, the largest rare disease diagnostic benchmark (49,760 cases across 4,597 diseases), where GPT-4o achieves 33.1% top-1 recall for screening and 64.2% for confirmation, with superior performance on genetically inherited and systemic/rheumatologic diseases.

## Reviews

The two review papers in this collection offer critical appraisals of LLM adoption in specific medical domains, highlighting both the transformative potential and the significant barriers to responsible implementation.

[12] provides a comprehensive review of LLM applications in otolaryngology, a specialty uniquely dependent on multimodal data (text, imaging, electrophysiology, video) and characterized by symptom-driven complexity. The review observes that most otolaryngology research has focused narrowly on question-answering tasks with limited clinical integration, whereas other specialties have demonstrated broader applications including automated phenotyping, administrative streamlining, and multimodal language model development. The authors call for moving beyond feasibility studies toward clinical trial-like validation, open-source model development, domain-specific fine-tuning, and secure multi-institutional deployment. This perspective resonates with findings from [6] and [10], which similarly reveal that current LLM performance, while impressive on standardized tests, falls short of the reliability needed for real clinical reasoning.

[13] examines the tension between user engagement and therapeutic alliance in AI psychotherapy applications, raising fundamental bioethical questions about whether LLM-mediated interactions can establish the genuine relational bonds essential to effective mental health treatment. This concern is amplified by [1], which found that different LLMs exhibit distinct communicative and ethical orientations—from procedural pragmatism to human-centered design—suggesting that the choice of model architecture may have profound implications for therapeutic outcomes. Together, these reviews underscore that technical capability alone is insufficient; clinical deployment of LLMs requires careful attention to domain-specific validation, ethical frameworks, and the nuanced relational dynamics of healthcare.

## Foundation Models

The five foundation model papers span domain-specific model specialization, vision-language architectures, multimedia communications, and novel model steering techniques, collectively illustrating the breadth of ongoing work to adapt and extend foundation model capabilities.

[14] investigates the impact of domain knowledge and data scale on open-source LLM specialization for anaerobic digestion, fine-tuning Llama3.1-8B to create LAD, which approaches GPT-4-level performance (0.67 vs. 0.68) in specialized tasks. Critically, the study demonstrates that training with only 50% of domain data leads to unstable hallucinations, underscoring the necessity of comprehensive domain datasets—a finding that contrasts with the few-shot capabilities celebrated in [16]. [15] proposes RTVCFM, a foundation-model-empowered framework for real-time video conferencing that integrates Video Time-Aware LLMs, Segment Anything Model 2, and diffusion-based fusion to achieve 95.6% compression ratio while maintaining 98.73% semantic similarity, addressing the growing traffic demands of interactive multimedia services. [16] introduces DrugLLM, a domain-specialized LLM for molecular optimization that features Functional Group Tokenization (FGT), achieving over 53% token compression compared to SMILES and surpassing GPT-4 in few-shot molecular generation. Two bioactive compounds were successfully validated through wet-lab experiments, demonstrating practical impact that goes beyond computational benchmarks. While [14] emphasizes the importance of large domain datasets, [16] showcases how domain-specific tokenization and pre-training strategies can enable effective few-shot performance. [17] presents AquaticCLIP, a contrastive language-image pretraining model for underwater scene understanding trained on a 2-million image-text paired dataset collected from diverse sources including YouTube and National Geographic. The model employs a prompt-guided vision encoder (PGVE) and achieves notable zero-shot performance improvements across multiple underwater CV tasks. [18] proposes State-wise Linear Modulation (SLim), a novel approach for steering LLM internal states to control affective and stylistic attributes in outputs, demonstrating that state-engineered outputs are more pronounced with subjective inputs—offering a complementary mechanism to prompt engineering for nuanced human-AI interaction.
