# Research Paper Report for 2026-06-01:2026-06-15

## Overall Summary

This reporting period spans eight publications that capture two dominant currents in AI-for-biology: the rapid proliferation of domain-specific foundation models, and a growing counter-movement of rigorous evaluation questioning whether naive scaling actually delivers biological insight. On the constructive side, UniBiomed [4] couples a multi-modal large language model with the Segment Anything Model, trained on 27 million image–region–text triplets, to produce grounded biomedical image interpretation that simultaneously generates diagnostic findings and segments targets, validated across 70 internal and 14 external datasets. ConnectoFM [5] pretrains on 1.7 million unlabeled electron microscopy images spanning six species and 25 subdomains using masked image modeling plus contrastive alignment, outperforming baselines trained on ten times more labeled data across 29 datasets. MMKD-CLIP [8] distills nine biomedical CLIP teachers into a single generalist vision–language model trained on 2.9 million image–text pairs across 26 modalities, and GLACIER [7] applies a complementary student–teacher strategy at the molecular level, fusing molecular graphs, SMILES strings, and physicochemical descriptors via a Finsler geometry-aware module before distilling MiniMol and MolFormer into a lightweight model. On the evaluative side, two independent studies challenge single-cell foundation model orthodoxy: [3] finds Geneformer/scGPT benefits are strongly task-dependent, with perturbation prediction possibly capturing little beyond simple summary statistics, while [6] pretrains 400 models across 6,400 experiments and observes no clear data scaling laws. Complementing these, Pepti-Agent [1] introduces a closed-loop, Model Context Protocol-based agent for multi-objective peptide design with per-step reproducibility traces, and [2] pairs protein language model embeddings with phylogenomics to annotate 99.9% of nearly 8,900 plant BAHD acyltransferases. Together, these papers signal a field maturing from capability demonstrations toward modularity, evidence provenance, interpretable grounding, and empirically disciplined scaling. Notably, knowledge distillation emerges as a recurring efficiency strategy [7], [8], and two venues (Nature Methods, Nature Communications) anchor the period's methodological rigor.

## AI Agents

This category features a single but methodologically significant contribution: Pepti-Agent [1], an agentic framework for therapeutic peptide design from A. Farimani's group. The work addresses a persistent engineering pain point in computational drug discovery—generative models and property predictors are typically "wired together as monolithic scripts that are difficult to inspect, extend, or reuse." Pepti-Agent resolves this by decomposing the design loop into independently inspectable Model Context Protocol (MCP) tools covering generation, property prediction, and single-residue mutation. A large language model controller invokes these tools while consulting live predictor output between calls, so that refinement is driven by each candidate's evolving multi-property state rather than by natural-language reasoning alone. This matters because therapeutic peptides face tightly coupled, competing constraints—solubility, hemolytic activity, and nonspecific surface fouling are governed by overlapping sequence features, so improving one property often degrades another. Technically, task-specific PeptideGPT models propose candidates, ProtBERT-based classifiers score solubility, hemolysis, and non-fouling, and two interchangeable mutation operators propose edits. Critically, a per-step trace of controller decisions, predictor outputs, and accepted mutations makes the framework a reproducible substrate for benchmarking multi-objective design strategies. Compared to GLACIER [7], which compresses property prediction into a single lightweight distilled model, Pepti-Agent instead externalizes the reasoning loop into an inspectable agent architecture—representing an orthogonal philosophy of transparency over efficiency. Likewise, while [2] delivers static functional annotations at genome scale, [1] prioritizes iterative, candidate-level optimization suitable for downstream experimental triage.

## Databases/Simulation

This category contains a landmark resource paper from G. Moghe's team: a systematic functional annotation of the BAHD acyltransferase enzyme family in plant genomes [2]. The work targets the well-known gap between genomic and functional annotation in plants. The authors assembled FuncZymeDB-BAHD, a curated database of 2,705 enzyme–acceptor–donor activities covering 336 BAHDs from 156 plant species—a 2-to-6-fold expansion over Swiss-Prot and prior compilations. Methodologically, the framework layers three components of increasing inference strength. First, FuncPred-OG maps queries to orthologous groups and previously characterized enzymes with high evidence provenance, enabling functional prediction for over half of BAHDs across 85 plant proteomes; five novel predictions were validated via in vitro assays. Second, for BAHDs lacking ortholog-based annotation, FuncPred-AI trains logistic-regression classifiers on protein language model embeddings, achieving Area-Under-the-Precision-Recall-curve metrics and correct-hit rates up to 93%, and yielding at least one probable donor/acceptor annotation for 99.9% (8,894/8,897) of BAHDs in the pan-plant dataset. Third, the complete workflow is deployed on a public web portal, reducing candidate selection from days to minutes. While foundation model papers in this collection [3]–[6] emphasize pretraining scale, [2] demonstrates a complementary path: careful data curation plus lightweight classifiers over pretrained embeddings can deliver near-complete annotation coverage for an entire enzyme family. The authors position the framework as a generalizable template for other enzyme families, aligning with the broader trend of combining phylogenomics with PLM representations.

## Foundation models

This is the largest and most thematically diverse category, comprising six papers that split into three clusters: critical evaluation of single-cell foundation models, biomedical imaging foundation models, and multimodal/distilled molecular models. The evaluative cluster is striking for its convergence: [3] systematically benchmarks Geneformer and scGPT on perturbation prediction and cell type annotation, finding that pretraining benefits are strongly task-dependent—substantial for annotation but limited for perturbation prediction—and that increasing model size can even be detrimental; synthetic-data comparisons suggest the models may capture little more than simple summary statistics. Independently, [6] pretrains 400 models on corpora drawn from 22.2 million cells and runs 6,400 experiments, showing that performance plateaus with datasets far smaller than current 100-million-cell atlases and that single-cell foundation models exhibit no clear data scaling laws. Together these papers argue for task-specific architectures and biologically informed priors over indiscriminate scaling. The imaging cluster includes UniBiomed [4], which integrates a multi-modal LLM with the Segment Anything Model for grounded interpretation—generating findings and segmenting targets—trained on 27 million triplets and validated on 70 internal and 14 external datasets; ConnectoFM [5], the first connectomics-specific foundation model, pretrained on 1.7 million EM images via masked image modeling and contrastive alignment, surpassing fully supervised baselines with only 10% labeled data across 29 datasets; and MMKD-CLIP [8], which distills nine biomedical CLIP teachers into one generalist model trained on 2.9 million image–text pairs across 26 modalities, evaluated on 58 datasets spanning six tasks. While [4] grounds interpretation via segmentation, [8] pursues breadth via multi-teacher distillation; [5] demonstrates that domain-tailored pretraining outperforms generic EM foundation models on fine structures like membranes, vesicles, and post-synaptic densities. Finally, GLACIER [7] extends the distillation paradigm to molecules, fusing graph, SMILES, and descriptor encoders through a Finsler geometry-aware module and distilling MiniMol/MolFormer teachers into a lightweight student with released code—an efficiency-focused counterpoint to the scaling critiques of [3] and [6].
