# Research Paper Report for 2026-03-01:2026-03-31

## Overall Summary

This reporting period showcases a striking convergence of two research currents in computational biology: the maturation of **biology-specific foundation models** and the rise of **agentic and neurosymbolic AI systems** that orchestrate these models within practical scientific workflows. A total of 11 papers were collected, spanning four categories — AI Agents (3), Foundation Models (6), Reviews (1), and Databases/Simulation (1) — with publication venues including two Nature Communications articles [7], [9], two Briefings in Bioinformatics papers [1], [10], six bioRxiv preprints, and one medRxiv preprint.

On the foundation-model side, the dominant theme is the injection of **biological inductive bias into transformer architectures**. GREmLN [4] embeds gene regulatory network and protein–protein interaction graph structure directly into the attention mechanism via graph signal processing, addressing the orderless nature of gene features and achieving parameter efficiency gains. CellVQ [7] scales to 68 million cells with 500 million parameters and introduces a Single-Cell Discretization module that renders cell embeddings interpretable as "cell codes," complemented by the CellVQ-Graph knowledge-graph tool. Sequence-level foundation models also advance across modalities: RNAElectra [6] replaces masked language modeling with ELECTRA-style replaced-token detection for dense supervision at nucleotide resolution; CDS-BART [8] applies a BART denoising objective to mRNA sequences; and PPLM [9] jointly encodes paired protein sequences for interaction, affinity, and contact prediction, outperforming ESM2 and structure-based baselines.

On the agentic side, ELISA [1] unifies single-cell expression embeddings, BioBERT-style retrieval, and LLM-mediated interpretation with a query classifier routing gene signatures versus natural language queries, achieving Cohen's d = 5.98 on gene-signature retrieval. A multi-agent LLM system [2] automates protein purification protocol extraction and optimization, while Monte Carlo Committee Simulation [3] simulates 14 persona-conditioned HTA panelists to predict drug reimbursement outcomes with calibrated confidence (ECE = 0.091). Complementary infrastructure contributions include a systematic review of >60 LLM agent systems [10] and h5adify [11], a neuro-symbolic toolkit using locally deployed LLMs for AnnData metadata harmonization. Collectively, these works signal a field-wide shift from monolithic prediction toward interpretable, grounded, and workflow-integrated AI.

## AI Agents

The AI Agents category this month demonstrates that agentic LLM systems have moved beyond generic chatbots into domain-grounded scientific discovery, though each paper takes a distinct architectural stance on the grounding problem. ELISA [1] tackles the disconnect between transcriptomic foundation models and natural language by coupling single-cell generative pretrained transformer expression embeddings with BioBERT-based semantic retrieval and LLM-mediated interpretation. Its automatic query classifier routes inputs among gene marker scoring, semantic matching, or reciprocal rank fusion depending on whether the query is a signature, a concept, or a mixture. Benchmarked across six diverse scRNA-seq datasets (inflammatory lung disease, pediatric/adult cancers, organoids, healthy tissue, neurodevelopment), ELISA significantly outperforms CellWhisperer, BM25, and random baselines (permutation p < 2×10⁻⁵ each; Cohen's d = 5.98 for mean reciprocal rank on gene-signature queries) and replicates published findings with a mean composite score of 0.88.

While [1] grounds an agent in expression data, [2] grounds one in the primary literature: a multi-agent LLM system that automates protein purification protocol design by identifying analogous proteins via sequence similarity search, extracting successful purification methodologies from primary sources, and cross-referencing failed protocols to generate optimization recommendations. Expert validation confirmed scientifically sound outputs, reducing analysis time from hours to minutes, though the authors flag the lack of programmatic open access to PDB primary citations as a structural limitation. In contrast, [3] applies agentic simulation to healthcare policy rather than wet-lab biology: 14 persona-conditioned LLM panelists with weighted voting and uncertainty quantification predict CDA-AMC reimbursement decisions. On temporally separated validation data (n=67, post knowledge cutoff), confident predictions achieved 93.2% accuracy (AUROC 0.817; ECE = 0.091), with 86.3% Hamming accuracy on condition-category prediction — a neurosymbolic forecast complementing, not replacing, human deliberation.

## Foundation Models

This month's foundation model papers collectively argue that generic transformer recipes must be rethought for each biological modality — graph structure for transcriptomics [4], [7], dense pretraining objectives for RNA [6], denoising objectives for mRNA [8], and paired encoding for protein complexes [9] — with [5] showing how LLMs can be composed with biophysical simulators for drug design. GREmLN [4] addresses the fundamental mismatch between sequentially structured transformers and orderless single-cell gene features by using graph signal processing to embed gene regulatory network and PPI graph structure directly into attention, capturing non-local dependencies and potential causal relationships; it outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on cell type annotation, graph structure understanding, and fine-tuned reverse perturbation prediction while enabling parameter-efficient architectures and faster convergence. CellVQ [7] improves on generalizability and interpretability along a different axis: pretraining on 68 million cells with 500 million parameters and challenging tasks, its Single-Cell Discretization (SCD) module converts sparse, heterogeneous data into interpretable "cell codes," and the plug-and-play CellVQ-Graph tool fuses these features with cell communication and annotation data into knowledge graphs for discovery.

For sequence modalities, RNAElectra [6] critiques masked language modeling's pretraining–downstream discrepancy (loss on few positions, artificial corruptions absent at inference) and substitutes ELECTRA-style replaced-token detection, training a discriminator over all positions on realistically corrupted RNAcentral sequences; it outperforms widely used RNA foundation models on structure, RNA–protein/RNA–RNA interactions, modification, and quantitative readouts such as translation efficiency and mRNA stability. CDS-BART [8] extends sequence denoising pretraining to mRNA (abstract unavailable in the source data). PPLM [9] jointly encodes paired protein sequences rather than single chains, spawning PPLM-PPI (state-of-the-art binary interaction prediction across species), PPLM-Affinity (beating ESM2 and structure-based methods on antibody–antigen and TCR–pMHC cases), and PPLM-Contact (surpassing existing inter-protein contact predictors). Finally, LLMsFold [5] chains a 70-billion-parameter LLaMA model generating drug-like SMILES under pocket-constrained prompts with Boltz-2 diffusion-based co-folding for affinity prediction and a reinforcement learning loop, demonstrating strong in silico candidates against ACVR1 (fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva) and CD19 (B-cell malignancies).

## Reviews

This category contains a single but high-impact systematic survey that contextualizes the entire agentic research program reflected elsewhere in this report. Zhang and colleagues [10] provide the first systematic synthesis of LLM-based agents across genomics, molecular biology, imaging, biomedical analysis, and automated bioinformatics workflows, analyzing more than 60 emerging systems — a scope that directly subsumes the concrete agent implementations reported this month, such as the expression-grounded ELISA agent [1], the multi-agent protein purification system [2], and the neurosymbolic metadata harmonizer h5adify [11]. Methodologically, the review organizes these systems within a unifying framework characterizing agentic traits — autonomous decision-making, external tool invocation, memory, and self-correction — providing a comparative vocabulary that individual system papers lack. Their comparative assessment finds early promise in multi-step analysis, heterogeneous evidence linking, and exploratory scientific tasks, but also identifies consistent failure modes that resonate with limitations acknowledged in the primary studies this month: unstable reasoning, limited biological grounding (a gap ELISA [1] explicitly targets with expression-grounded embeddings), retrieval misalignment (a risk mitigated in [2] via primary-source protocol extraction and cross-referencing), and barriers to reproducibility and biosafety. The survey closes by charting opportunities for trustworthy and collaborative biological agents, including multimodal integration, closed-loop experimental design, and robust evaluation practices — directions that align with the calibrated uncertainty quantification demonstrated by Monte Carlo Committee Simulation [3]. Published in Briefings in Bioinformatics (Q1) and already accumulating citations, this review serves as the field's reference map for the agentic turn in biological discovery.

## Databases/Simulation

This category features one infrastructure-focused contribution addressing a prerequisite for the entire foundation-model ecosystem: reliable, harmonized training data. h5adify [11] recognizes that as public single-cell and spatial transcriptomics repositories grow, the bottleneck for atlas-scale integration has shifted from data generation to metadata heterogeneity — inconsistent column naming, partial annotations, and mixed gene identifier conventions that break reproducible merging, downstream benchmarking, and reuse in foundation model training (directly relevant to models such as GREmLN [4] and CellVQ [7], which depend on large-scale pretraining corpora). The toolkit is explicitly neurosymbolic, combining deterministic biological inference with locally deployed LLMs to perform metadata field discovery, gene identifier harmonization, optional paper-aware extraction, and consensus resolution with explicit uncertainty logging. A notable methodological contribution is its systematic benchmarking of four open-weight model families served through Ollama (Gemma, Llama, Mistral, and Qwen), demonstrating that small, locally run models achieve high semantic accuracy in metadata resolution with low hallucination rates and modest computational requirements — a privacy-preserving alternative to cloud APIs that echoes the reproducibility concerns raised in the agent review [10] and the open-access limitations flagged by the protein purification system [2]. In controlled simulations injecting annotation noise into single-cell and Visium-like spatial datasets, harmonization measurably improved integration benchmarking and reduced spurious batch effects, while application to sex-annotated glioblastoma datasets recovered biologically coherent microenvironmental patterns and cell-type-specific genomic differences not explained by differential expression alone — concrete evidence that semantic cleanup yields genuine biological dividends.
