# Research Paper Report for 2026-04-01:2026-04-30

## Overall Summary

This reporting period captures three bioRxiv preprints that together illustrate the maturing role of large AI models—agentic LLMs, generative sequence models, and multimodal foundation models—across the life sciences. A unifying theme is the movement of LLM technology from text-centric tasks into structured biological evidence synthesis and molecular generation, coupled with a growing emphasis on rigorous evaluation of model reliability.

Paper [1] provides a sober audit of agentic LLMs for high-volume protein functional annotation. Testing three agent configurations (Claude App, Claude Code CLI, and Codex App, each run three times) against a fixed task of 73 orthogroups and 1,705 sequences with deterministic local evidence (Swiss-Prot BLASTP, Pfam/HMMER, DeepTMHMM, SignalP), the study finds that while coverage was complete, within-method exact tier agreement was only moderate (means of 0.516–0.562), and per-run high-confidence calls varied from 0 to 12. Documented failure modes—such as elevating pentapeptide-repeat orthogroups on motif evidence alone and taking low-complexity BLAST labels at face value—motivate a best-practice workflow in which agents draft annotations only after deterministic evidence tables are generated, with explicit scoring rules, provenance tracking, replication, and expert review.

Paper [2] presents H2O, a foundation model that bridges histopathology and spatial multi-omics by integrating Vision Transformers with LLMs via contrastive learning, trained on 1.3 million paired H&E–spatial patches across 25 organs and cancer types. H2O infers spatial transcriptomic and proteomic landscapes from routine H&E slides, outperforming state-of-the-art models on three cancer benchmarks and recovering the MIF-CD74/CD44 signaling axis directly from images—demonstrating cost-effective, scalable molecular profiling.

Paper [3] introduces sdAbs-LLM, which trains five compact transformer architectures (Llama-4-, Gemma-3-, DeepSeek-V3-, Mistral-, and Nemotron-inspired variants) from scratch on 15 million Observed Antibody Space sequences for de novo VH single-domain antibody design. Structural validation via AlphaFold-2, Boltz-2, RoseTTAFold-2, and ESMFold yielded mean pLDDT scores of 92.88–93.77 with no significant inter-architecture differences, and a proof-of-concept agentic evaluation pipeline using the Model Context Protocol with Claude Sonnet 4.6.

Collectively, these papers advance both capability (cross-modal inference, generative design) and trustworthiness (reproducibility auditing, immunogenicity screening), while noting limitations including single-seed experiments [3] and persistent biological overinterpretation by skill-enabled agents [1].

## Benchmarks

This category features a systematic benchmark of agentic large language models applied to complex, high-volume protein functional annotation [1]. The study is methodologically notable for its controlled, fully deterministic evidence design: three agent configurations—Claude App (Claude Opus 4.7), Claude Code CLI (Claude Opus 4.7 with Claude Scientific Skills), and Codex App (GPT-5.4 with Claude Scientific Skills)—were each run three times on a verbatim prompt covering 73 orthogroup FASTA files (1,705 protein sequences), supported by identical local evidence comprising Swiss-Prot BLASTP output, Pfam/HMMER domain hits, DeepTMHMM topology predictions, and SignalP secretion predictions. This produced nine auditable runs evaluated for coverage, biological correctness, missing evidence, hallucinated or over-specific annotations, and within-method consistency. The quantitative findings are sobering: normalized calcification-relevance calls were only moderately reproducible, with within-method exact tier agreement ranging 0.397–0.685 for Claude App (mean 0.562), 0.342–0.740 for Claude Code (mean 0.516), and 0.411–0.630 for Codex App (mean 0.539), and high-confidence call counts varying from 0 to 12 across runs. The curated final table retained 3 high-confidence, 9 moderate, 18 watchlist, and 43 low-relevance orthogroups, with sulfatase (OG0017138), sulfotransferase (OG0020703), and FG-GAP/integrin-like (OG0018986) families as the most robust candidates. While skill-enabled agents improved file handling, evidence traceability, and reproducibility of computational checking, they did not eliminate biological overinterpretation—a key limitation. The paper's practical contribution is a best-practice workflow: deterministic evidence tables first, agent drafting second, with explicit scoring rules, provenance columns, run-to-run replication, and expert review of high-impact claims.

## Foundation Models

This category contains two preprints deploying foundation-model architectures at both ends of the biological modeling spectrum: cross-modal molecular inference from tissue images and generative de novo protein design. Paper [2] presents H2O (Histopathology to Omics), a generalist framework that integrates Vision Transformers with Large Language Models through contrastive learning to align histological morphology with semantic molecular knowledge. Trained on 1.3 million paired H&E–spatial patches spanning 25 organs and cancer types, H2O directly infers spatial transcriptomics and spatial proteomics landscapes from routine H&E slides, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art models across three cancer benchmarks and generalizing to fetal/paediatric thymus, metastatic lymph node, and breast cancer cohorts spanning 3D spatial frameworks and integrative multi-omics. A striking biological validation is the recovery of the MIF-CD74/CD44 signaling axis directly from H&E images, positioning histopathology as a computationally scalable portal to spatially resolved molecular profiling. Whereas [2] focuses on predictive inference across modalities, [3] (sdAbs-LLM) targets generative design: five compact transformer variants inspired by Llama-4, Gemma-3, DeepSeek-V3, Mistral 7B, and NVIDIA Nemotron-3 were trained from scratch on 15 million Observed Antibody Space sequences and fine-tuned on disease-stratified repertoires (SARS-CoV-2, HIV, HER2, Ebola). Generative fidelity was uniform across architectures (diversity 0.507–0.516; novelty 0.925–0.977), and mean pLDDT from AlphaFold-2, Boltz-2, RoseTTAFold-2, and ESMFold (92.88–93.77) showed no significant differences (Kruskal–Wallis H=2.06, p>0.05), suggesting data and scale dominate at this regime—a limitation acknowledged as single-seed. Docking energies of −36.34 to −65.60 kcal/mol, CDR-H3 identity of 0–29%, 77–90% VH germline content, and no strong MHC-II binders via NetMHCIIpan 4.3 confirmed developability. Notably, [3] also introduces a Model Context Protocol agentic evaluation pipeline, complementing the reliability auditing of agentic systems in [1].
